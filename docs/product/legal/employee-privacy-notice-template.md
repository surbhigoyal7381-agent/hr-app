## Part B — The full notice

# How {Company} uses your personal data

**Version {1.0} · {date} · Also available in: {हिंदी / other languages}**

## 1. In short

- **{Company} decides** what personal data about you is used and why. It is your employer.
- **Alvoraa runs the software** that {Company} uses for HR. Alvoraa only uses your data to
  run that software for {Company}.
- We use your data to **employ you**: to keep your records, mark your attendance, manage your
  leave, pay you, and review your work.
- **The mobile attendance app is your choice.** It takes a photo and your location only when
  you press Check In or Check Out. If you do not agree, you can mark attendance another way,
  and nothing bad happens to you.
- Your data is **stored in France**.
- You can **ask to see or correct** your data, and **complain** if you are unhappy. Contact
  {Grievance officer name} at {email}.

## 2. Who decides, and who does the work

| Who | Role under India's data protection law | What that means |
|---|---|---|
| **{Company legal name}**, {address} | **Data Fiduciary** | Decides why and how your data is used. Answers to you and to the Data Protection Board |
| **{Alvoraa legal entity name}**, {address} | **Data Processor** | Runs the HR software for {Company}. Uses your data only on {Company}'s instructions, under a written contract |

`[LAWYER TO CONFIRM — roles, and whether Alvoraa must be named.]`

**What we do not do**

- We **do not track your location between check-ins**. The attendance app does not run in
  the background.
- We **do not use your photo to recognise your face**, and we do not send it to any other
  company or to any AI system.
- We **do not use AI to set your rating.** A named person decides.
- We **do not** read your phone's contacts, gallery, messages, phone number, SIM details or
  device identity numbers.

## 4. Our legal basis

| Data | Legal basis | |
|---|---|---|

| Photo and location collected through the **mobile app** | **Your consent** — section 6 | You choose. You can withdraw at any time. See section 5. `[LAWYER TO CONFIRM]` |

## 5. The mobile attendance app — your choice

*(Delete this section if {Company} does not use the app.)*

**Using the app is up to you.**

- Before the app is set up, it shows you a short notice and asks you to tick **"I agree"**.
  If you do not tick it, the app is not set up and collects nothing.
- **If you say no, mark attendance {the usual way — for example: at the reception register /
  on the attendance machine / by telling your supervisor, who records it}.** This way does not
  need a photo or your location.
- **Saying no will not affect your pay, your job, your review or how you are treated.**
  `[LAWYER TO CONFIRM — this is what makes consent "free". HR must make sure it is true in
  practice.]`

**What we record when you agree**

- The date and time you agreed, the version of the notice, the language, and which phone.
- If the notice changes, the app asks you to read it and agree again before your next
  check-in. Until you do, use {the usual way}.

**How to stop (withdraw your consent)**

- In the app: **Settings › Remove this phone from {Company}**. You need the internet for this.
  `[PRODUCT GAP — designed, not built yet.]`
- Or tell HR, who can stop the phone at once.
- Stopping is as easy as starting. After you stop, the app collects nothing more.

**What happens to what was already recorded**

- Your attendance times stay in your HR record. They are needed to pay you.
- Photos and exact locations taken through the app are **{deleted within {X} days of you
  stopping / deleted when the normal {photo retention days}-day period ends}**.
  `[LAWYER TO CONFIRM — section 8(7) says data should be erased when consent is withdrawn,
  unless a law requires keeping it. PRODUCT GAP — the product cannot yet delete one person's
  photos when they withdraw; it only deletes photos older than the retention period.]`
- Withdrawing does not make earlier processing unlawful (section 6(5)).
