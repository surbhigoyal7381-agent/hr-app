Performance reviews
A review takes its own copy of each objective and KPI, so the numbers it was decided on stay put. These three settings say when those numbers close, how long the originals stay locked, and what happens to an item taken out of a review.
After this point, later progress and evidence no longer change the review.
While a review holds an objective or KPI, its name, target, weight and period cannot change. Use 0 to keep it locked until the review is finished.
An item that already has a rating is always kept and marked removed, whichever setting is chosen.
Attendance rules
Every company sets its own rules. The first two switches are off until HR switches them on.
When on, HR sets this company's own rule: how late counts, free lapses a week, deduction per lapse, which leave is used first, then pay, and which grades are exempt.
When on, each appraisal cycle can include attendance with its own weight.
When on, a manager can ask HR to fix attendance — and later leave — for the people who report directly to them. HR still decides every request. When off, only the employee can ask about their own day, and HR can fix any day.
Leave this empty for no limit. Counted in working days, using the employee's own holiday list, so weekends and holidays do not use up the time. A manager who asks later than this is told to go to HR instead. It does not limit HR, and it does not limit an employee asking about their own day — HR can still fix any day. Use 0 to let a manager ask about today only.
Key Result Areas
KRAs are the areas of responsibility that belong to a role. They come from the employee's appraisal template, so every objective can be traced back to what the job is for.
When on, an objective cannot be activated without a KRA. Drafts are still allowed, so nobody is blocked while HR assigns an appraisal template.
